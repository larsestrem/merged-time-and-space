Asteroid belt

What is the asteroid belt?

A ring of leftover rock between Mars and Jupiter. It is not the wreckage of a planet. Jupiter’s gravity never let the rubble gather into one world. The dark gaps in it are resonances — the same tug, at the same point, over and over.

Rubble that never became a world

The belt’s total mass is about 3% of the Moon’s. Ceres, the largest body in it, is itself a dwarf planet. Switch the belt on in the solar system simulator and you are looking at leftover building material, not a destroyed planet.

The gaps are Jupiter’s doing

An asteroid whose year is a simple fraction of Jupiter’s gets the same tug at the same point of its orbit, over and over, until it is pushed out. Those empty lanes are the Kirkwood gaps. The same gravity that kept the belt from becoming a planet is still editing it.

Related questions

Why does Jupiter have so many moons? Gravity’s reach grows with mass, and Jupiter has more of it than anything in the solar system except the Sun. A wide region around it is a place where a small body stays with Jupiter instead of orbiting the Sun on its own.

How does an orbit work? At every instant a planet has a velocity — where it is already going — and gravity adds a small pull straight toward the Sun. Bend a path by the same small amount at every step and it closes. That closed path is the orbit.

Why don’t planets fall into the Sun? They are falling. They are also moving sideways. By the time a planet has fallen, the Sun is no longer where it was falling toward. Falling and missing, without end, is an orbit. There is no outward force holding them up.
