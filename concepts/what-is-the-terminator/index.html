Terminator

What is the terminator?

The terminator is the line between day and night — the circle on Earth where the Sun sits on the horizon. Sunrise is where you cross it going into the light; sunset is where you cross it going out.

A circle, drawn as a wave

On a globe the terminator is a circle: everywhere the Sun is exactly on the horizon. Half the planet is in front of it, half behind. Because sunlight arrives almost in parallel, that split is always half-and-half, whatever the date.

The map at the top of this site is flat, so the circle is stretched into a curve. Near an equinox the curve runs pole to pole. Near a solstice it leans, and one polar cap stays in daylight while the other stays dark.

How fast it moves

The line itself is not a thing racing over the ground. Earth is turning under a Sun that, for this purpose, sits still. At the equator that turn is about 1,670 km/h — 15 degrees of longitude per hour, which is why time zones are an hour wide.

This map solves the terminator per meridian: for each longitude, where is the Sun at altitude 0°? That is one equation in latitude. The shaded night is those latitudes closed toward the dark pole. Twilight is the same solve at −18°.

Related questions

What is the subsolar point? It is the one place on Earth where the Sun is straight overhead right now — where a flagpole casts no shadow. It is the yellow dot on the day/night map, and it is where the Sun-centre to Earth-centre line hits the surface.

What is twilight? Twilight is the time when the Sun is below the horizon but still lighting the sky. This map draws it as a soft band: from the terminator down to where the Sun is 18° below — the edge of astronomical night.

What is Earth’s axial tilt? Earth’s spin axis is not upright. It leans about 23.4° from the vertical to its orbit. That lean is why we have tropics, polar circles, and seasons — and it barely changes from one year to the next.

Why is this map flat? This map gives every degree of latitude and longitude the same length on the page, so meridians are straight and the terminator is honest. The cost is size: land near the poles is stretched. The picture says so, in numbers from the drawing.
