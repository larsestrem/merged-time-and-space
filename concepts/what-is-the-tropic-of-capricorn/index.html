Tropic of Capricorn

What is the Tropic of Capricorn?

It is the circle at 23.4° south — the southern twin of the Tropic of Cancer. The Sun stands straight overhead there once a year, on the winter solstice, and never south of it.

The same limit, going south

Whatever the Tropic of Cancer is for the north, the Tropic of Capricorn is for the south. Earth’s axis leans about 23.4°. That lean never grows, so the Sun’s overhead point never crosses either tropic.

Between the two tropics, every place gets the Sun straight up on two days each year — once on the way north, once on the way back. Outside them, a vertical pole always casts some noon shadow, even on the longest day.

December, not a different Earth

Nothing about Earth changes between June and December except which end of the axis leans toward the light. When it is summer in Chile it is winter in Canada, on the same planet, at the same distance from the Sun. Distance is not the season.

The name Capricorn is the same kind of fossil as Cancer: the December-solstice Sun used to stand in that constellation. Precession moved the stars. The latitude stayed with the tilt.

Related questions

What is the Tropic of Cancer? It is the circle at 23.4° north. The Sun is straight overhead there once a year, on the summer solstice, and never north of it. That number is Earth’s tilt, not a climate zone someone drew.

What is the subsolar point? It is the one place on Earth where the Sun is straight overhead right now — where a flagpole casts no shadow. It is the yellow dot on the day/night map, and it is where the Sun-centre to Earth-centre line hits the surface.

What is Earth’s axial tilt? Earth’s spin axis is not upright. It leans about 23.4° from the vertical to its orbit. That lean is why we have tropics, polar circles, and seasons — and it barely changes from one year to the next.

Why do we have seasons? Not because Earth gets closer to the Sun. When it is summer in the United States it is winter in Australia, on the same planet, at the same distance. Seasons happen because the axis is tilted 23.4° and keeps pointing the same way all year.
