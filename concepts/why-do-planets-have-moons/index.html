How moons form

Why do planets have moons, and how are they made?

Three ways, and you can tell them apart. Some moons form from the same disc as their planet. Some form from the debris of a giant impact. Some are captured — they were going past and got stuck. Triton’s backward orbit on Neptune is the capture giveaway. Earth’s Moon on the Earth and Moon view is the impact story.

Disc, smash, or grab

A disc moon sits on a tidy, forward, nearly circular orbit near the equator — Io, Europa, Titan. An impact moon can be huge compared with its planet: our Moon is a quarter of Earth’s width; Charon is half of Pluto’s. A captured moon often comes in on a tilted or backward path, like Triton.

The same falling-and-missing that keeps a planet beside the Sun keeps a moon beside a planet. More mass, a larger volume of space where “beside the planet” wins.

Earth’s Moon is the odd one in the inner system

A Mars-sized body hit the young Earth. Debris went into orbit and became the Moon. That much is broadly agreed. The puzzle is that the Moon’s rocks look almost identical to Earth’s, while most impact models make a moon out of the impactor. Getting a Moon that looks like Earth’s mantle out of a collision with something else is still an unsolved modelling problem.

Related questions

Why does one of Neptune’s moons go the other way? Most moons travel the same way their planet spins, because they formed from the same spinning disc. Triton goes the other way, so it did not form next to Neptune. It was captured, almost certainly from the Kuiper belt.

Why does Jupiter have so many moons? Gravity’s reach grows with mass, and Jupiter has more of it than anything in the solar system except the Sun. A wide region around it is a place where a small body stays with Jupiter instead of orbiting the Sun on its own.

Why does the Moon change shape? The Moon makes no light of its own. One half is always lit by the Sun. Phases are your changing view of that lit half as the Moon goes around Earth — about 29.5 days from full moon to full moon.
