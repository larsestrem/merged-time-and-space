Day and Night Map

See where sunlight reaches Earth at this moment—or choose any date and time. The bright half is day, the dark half is night, and the soft boundary is twilight.

The sun is overhead at 7.9° N, 118.6° E — this is the subsolar point. This time of year the sun is moving from summer toward fall, and days in the northern half of the world are getting shorter because Earth is tilting away from the sun in Earth’s orbit.

Day & Night Map Instructions

This map solves which half of Earth faces the Sun at the instant shown above the slider. Bright areas have the Sun above the horizon, dark areas have it below, and the soft band is twilight.

Drag the slider to choose any instant in the year. Use the arrow-step menu to decide whether the arrow buttons move one hour, one day, or one week. Play runs through the year, Now returns to the current moment, and the seasonal buttons reveal how the day/night boundary changes across the year.

The yellow marker is the subsolar point, where the Sun is straight overhead. The moon marker is where the Moon stands overhead, drawn in its phase at that moment—it can be a daytime Moon. The dashed gold lines are the tropics, and the curved boundary is the terminator. Its daily sweep also shows why longitude matters to time zones.

Yellow markerSubsolar point — a flagpole there casts no shadow.
Moon markerOverhead, in the phase of that moment. One orbit around Earth takes 27.3 days.
Soft bandTwilight, widest toward the poles.
