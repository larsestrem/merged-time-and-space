# Ideas saved for later

Owner's parking lot. Nothing here is committed work — each idea graduates by
being asked for. When one is built, move its line to PROGRESS.md and delete it
here. Additions welcome any time; the bar for adding is low on purpose.

## The owner's two big ones (August 2026)

- **How space created time — the full treatment.** The concept page
  (/concepts/who-invented-time/) is the seed. The bigger version is a whole
  thread through the site: every unit of time traced to the observation it
  came from, with the simulator view that shows that observation beside it.
  Could become the connective tissue between the Time and Space sections —
  the site's own thesis, made navigable. Possible shape: a "How the sky
  became the clock" trail that walks day → month → seasons → year → hour →
  minute in order, each stop one existing page.

- **The scientists, and what each one actually did.** One page per person,
  action-first: not a biography but the specific act — what they looked at,
  what they noticed, what it let everyone do next. Candidates whose work the
  site can DEMONSTRATE with its own tools (the test for inclusion — a
  scientist page here should have a live drawing under it, not a portrait):
  - Eratosthenes — measured the Earth with two shadows and a well. (Also a
    TOOL idea: two towns, two shadow lengths, the class computes Earth's
    circumference — the site already knows every town's sun angle.)
  - Galileo — pointed the telescope at Jupiter; four moons that plainly did
    not orbit Earth. The moons card already draws them.
  - Kepler — turned Tycho's tables into the three laws; the solar system
    simulator IS his laws running.
  - Newton — the cannonball thought experiment; the orbital-velocity
    simulator IS that cannonball.
  - Copernicus — moved the center; the view-ladder's "step back" is his move.
  - Foucault — a pendulum that shows the Earth turning under it.
  - Harrison — the carpenter whose clock solved longitude (time IS
    longitude: every 15° of Earth's turn is one hour — ties clocks to maps).
  - Anning, Herschel (either), Cannon/Leavitt — stretch candidates once the
    format works.

## Time-and-space connective ideas

- **Time is longitude.** Why knowing the time at Greenwich tells you where
  you are; the sea-clock story; 15° per hour derived on the world-clock map.
- **Why 60? Why 12?** Babylonian base-60 and Egyptian finger-joint counting —
  where minutes, seconds and the 12-hour halves actually come from. Could be
  its own concept or fold into the who-invented-time page's deeper band.
- **The day is getting longer.** Earth's spin is slowing (leap seconds,
  ancient eclipse records, coral growth rings recording ~400-day years).
  Strong "deeper" material; a good page once it can cite figures carefully.
- **Your age on Mars.** Birthday-to-Mars-years converter — every figure
  derived from the planets' real periods, already in planets.mjs.
- **A sundial for your town.** The equation of time drawn as the analemma
  for the reader's own latitude; why clock noon and sun noon disagree today
  by the amount the page computes. (The owner's wishlist already names it.)
- **Calendar systems.** Why February is short, why the leap month existed,
  how lunar, solar and lunisolar calendars each solved the same mismatch.
  Curriculum-safe comparative material; needs careful, quoted-facts writing.
- **Star clocks.** Sidereal time made visible: why a star rises 4 minutes
  earlier each night, and how that difference IS the year. The sidereal-day
  concept exists; this is the interactive.
