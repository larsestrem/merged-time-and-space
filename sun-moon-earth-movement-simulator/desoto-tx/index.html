    DeSoto is the marker on the globe — on the daylit half at the time shown, riding the dotted circle its spot traces as the Earth turns. The moon is 178° from the sun in the sky then, and that angle is the phase: full moon. You are looking down on the Earth from far above its orbit, from the north — the side you are on — with sunlight arriving from the top left, so the half of the Earth facing that way is the half having its day. Sizes and distances are not to scale; the directions are.

    What DeSoto’s latitude does to the picture

    At 32.6° north, DeSoto has a moderate swing through the year — 14 h 18 m of daylight at the longest day and 10 h 0 m at the shortest — and the sun never quite reaches straight overhead.
